# Asian Optimist Index: launch proposal for 2026

The 2026 IODA Asian & Oceanian Optimist Championship should be the **anchor event** for a new SailorPath Asian Optimist Index. IODA lists the continental Optimist championship in Colombo, Sri Lanka, from 12 to 19 December 2026, and has published its Notice of Race. [1] [2]

Do not call the first version an “Asian ranking.” That label suggests IODA, a federation, or the event organiser has endorsed a formal ranking system. Call it the **SailorPath Asian Optimist Index** and describe it as a transparent, source-backed measure of recent results. Keep the official championship result separate and untouched.

## The public structure

Publish three related views rather than one overloaded leaderboard.

**Asian Championship standings** should show the official final overall result of the 2026 IODA Asian & Oceanian Championship exactly as published. It is the authoritative answer to who won the event. SailorPath adds result-source links, sailor profiles and context, but does not recalculate it.

**SailorPath Asian Optimist Index** should show a rolling 12-month performance measure, starting immediately after the championship. Its main job is to compare a sailor’s continental-championship performance with verified form at selected Asian events.

**Country views** should remain separate. A sailor can lead the Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand or Philippines view without needing to travel internationally. This avoids forcing a cross-border standard onto sailors who mainly compete at home.

Before Colombo, show a **Road to Colombo** directory rather than a numbered Asia-wide leaderboard. Display sailor profiles, verified regatta history, home-country standing and the number of eligible events. This builds the directory and result base without pretending that a thin set of national results is a fair regional ranking.

## Recommended 2026 formula

Use a 100-point index, issued only after the Colombo final results are official:

> **Asian Optimist Index = 60% Championship Performance + 40% Recent Verified Form**

The championship should carry the most weight because it puts sailors from across the region on the same race course, under the same rules, at the same time. That gives it much stronger cross-country comparability than a national event.

Calculate a sailor’s **Championship Performance** from their final overall placing, normalised for the validated final fleet:

> **Championship Performance = 100 × (fleet size − final place) ÷ (fleet size − 1)**

First place therefore earns 100; last classified place earns 0. Use the published overall final classification, not a prize list, a fleet split, a social post, or a provisional score sheet.

Calculate **Recent Verified Form** from the sailor’s best three eligible non-championship results in the 12 months before the championship. First calculate the same 0–100 fleet-normalised result score for each event. Then apply these weights:

> **Recent Verified Form = 70% × best event + 20% × second-best event + 10% × third-best event**

A missing second or third event is zero. This recognises participation and consistency without awarding points simply for turning up. A poor or unverified event does not improve a sailor’s score.

## Event eligibility and weight caps

An approved event must have an organiser-published final overall result, a source link, a known event date, race count, fleet size, and a clear Optimist class/fleet. Results must be visually reconciled from the original PDF or scoring export before import.

The index should accept three types of evidence. The continental championship is the anchor. Recognised international youth events in Asia can count at full non-championship value when they have at least 20 classified sailors and entries from at least three nationalities. National championships and national opens can count, but their individual result score is capped at **70 points** and only one result from a sailor’s home country can appear in their best three. Club and development events remain visible on profiles but do not affect the Asia-wide index at launch.

This is deliberately conservative. It prevents a sailor from building a leading regional score solely through a large domestic calendar, while still giving national events a meaningful role. Malaysia’s LIRP and LIYSC events are good candidates for the international tier once their final sheets, fleet size and nationality data are verified. The MSA–NSC–MILO National Open is a strong national-tier candidate.

Only **overall/final Optimist classifications** should count initially. Do not double-count an overall result together with division, girls, novice or age-group prize sheets. Those category results belong on the sailor profile and can support future age- or gender-specific views.

## Participation should be visible, not artificially rewarded

Show an **activity panel** alongside the index: eligible starts, distinct host countries, most recent verified result and championship status. These are useful signals for families, coaches and scouts, but they should not earn direct index points. Direct attendance bonuses would favour sailors with larger travel budgets over sailors with better racing results.

Show an **index confidence label** instead of hiding the limitation of sparse participation:

- **High confidence:** championship result plus three eligible non-championship results.
- **Established:** championship result plus one or two eligible results.
- **Championship only:** championship result, no supporting eligible form.
- **Emerging:** verified profile and results, but no championship result; show in the directory, not the numbered index.

The label gives the audience an honest view of coverage without penalising talented sailors who have less access to travel.

## Practical launch sequence

First, import and visually validate the overall Optimist results for the 2026 Malaysian LIRP and LIYSC. Keep each as **MAS**, source-backed and non-ranking for the Singapore leaderboard. Use them to populate the Road to Colombo profiles.

Second, add one or two other countries only when their official final result sources are equally strong. Do not rush into a pan-Asian table built from uneven country coverage.

Third, import the official Colombo overall results after the regatta finishes and publish the official championship standings immediately. Once the source has been checked and any protest-related amendments are final, calculate the first 2026 Asian Optimist Index.

Finally, display the formula, included events, source links, last-calculated date and a simple correction process on the index page. Version the rules as **Asian Optimist Index v1.0** so changes in later seasons are visible rather than silently rewriting history.

## What this gives SailorPath

This approach makes the December championship a credible regional launch moment. It gives sailors who stay mostly national a useful profile and country context, gives internationally active sailors a measurable form signal, and avoids presenting a commercial, incomplete dataset as an official continental ranking.
