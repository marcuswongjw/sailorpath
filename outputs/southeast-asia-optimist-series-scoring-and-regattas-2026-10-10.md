# Southeast Asia Optimist Series — scoring system and candidate regattas

**Prepared:** 10 October 2026
**Status:** Recommendation for a pilot season. No event is included, no policy is approved, and no leaderboard is activated by this document.

## Recommended scoring system

Call the pilot product the **Southeast Asia Optimist Series — Provisional**. It should be a declared season with a short, published list of eligible events—not a ranking generated from every result SailorPath can find.

Use the organiser’s final published overall result as the source of each sailor’s finish. SailorPath should never recalculate an organiser’s race scores or discards. It should convert the final event placing into a transparent season score.

### Season shape

Start with **six declared events across Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand**, with no more than two host-country events. Count each sailor’s **best three** event scores.

A sailor must have a valid score from at least **two** events to appear in the ranked table. A sailor with one valid event may appear in the results directory, but not in the season standings. There should be no rule requiring overseas travel in the first season: access to travel is not a measure of ability.

All eligible events are worth the same in season one. Do not introduce subjective “event strength,” host-country, club, or nationality bonuses. A larger or more prestigious event can be considered for the calendar only through the pre-season eligibility decision.

### Event eligibility

An event can count only when all of the following are true:

1. It was named on the season calendar before its first race.
2. SailorPath retains the official notice, final result source, event location, organiser, and reviewer record.
3. It has at least **20 sailors in one published, compatible Optimist overall cohort** and at least **four completed races**.
4. The organiser publishes an overall order that covers the whole eligible cohort. When a regatta splits Gold and Silver fleets, SailorPath needs an official combined overall result; separate fleet results alone are not enough.
5. The published result has passed SailorPath’s normal score, discard, sailor-identity, and division review.

The 20-sailor threshold is deliberately conservative. It stops a five-boat local division from carrying the same season value as a large open fleet, while still allowing a developing national fleet to be visible in SailorPath’s results directory.

### Event-score formula

For a sailor with final position `P` in an eligible cohort of `N` starters:

```text
Event score = 100 × (N + 1 − P) ÷ N
```

Round the score to two decimal places. The winner always receives 100.00. The formula rewards a higher finishing percentile and does not require SailorPath to make an unprovable judgment about which national fleet is “stronger.”

For example, in a 20-sailor cohort, first scores 100.00, fifth scores 80.00, tenth scores 55.00, and twentieth scores 5.00. In a 60-sailor cohort, first scores 100.00, fifth scores 93.33, fifteenth scores 76.67, and thirtieth scores 51.67.

A sailor shown in the official overall result after a DNF, DNS, OCS, BFD, or other race-level code uses the organiser’s final event position. A sailor without a final overall position receives 0.00 for that event. The zero is retained in the audit trail but does not help the sailor’s best-three total.

### Season standing and ties

The season score is the sum of the three highest event scores. A sailor who has only two eligible events is ranked with the sum of those two scores; this lets the first season show a meaningful table without forcing a fourth trip.

Break a season tie in this order:

1. Higher total across **all** eligible event scores before best-three discards.
2. Higher single event score, then second-highest score, and so on.
3. If still equal, publish the sailors as tied. Do not use alphabetical order, nationality, age, or an opaque committee decision to manufacture a winner.

Every public standing should link to its event-score ledger: source event, final place, eligible fleet size, points, whether the score counted, and the policy version. A correction to an event result must produce a replacement calculation run rather than silently rewriting a previous published table.

### Why this is the right first-season model

It is simple enough for families and coaches to check, uses only information that official results normally contain, and gives SailorPath no discretionary “strength” multiplier to defend. The organiser’s own series scoring still determines the event’s final placings; SailorPath only turns that published placing into a comparable season score.

Do **not** use a global 12-month rolling ranking for the first regional release. A rolling ranking makes it much harder to explain why an event enters or expires, and it amplifies uneven data coverage across countries. Run one fixed pilot season, publish its policy before racing begins, then improve the rule using real outcome data.

## Regattas to approach outside Singapore

These are **candidates**, not commitments. Each must pass the event-eligibility checks above and the organiser must agree to a result handoff or review path.

### Malaysia — first anchors

**5th Langkawi International Youth Sailing Championship** is the strongest immediate Malaysian candidate. Its 2026 Notice of Race set the event at the National Sailing Centre in Langkawi from 24–28 June, invited national and international competitors, included Optimist U16, scheduled up to 12 races, and used a low-point event system. It is a good SEA Series candidate because it is explicitly youth- and Optimist-facing, has an identifiable organiser, and has a document trail suitable for review. [1]

**MSA–NSC–MILO National Open Championship** should be the second Malaysian event to approach. The 39th edition was held in Langkawi on 18–22 November 2025 as part of the Malaysian Ranking Circuit. Its Notice of Race included Optimist overall, age-group, and girls divisions, welcomed international sailors, and scheduled 12 races. Include only a later edition after a current Notice of Race and final overall result are available; the 2025 document shows a credible recurring event pattern, not automatic eligibility for a future year. [2]

### Thailand — one national anchor and one international-style youth event

**Thailand National Sailing Championship** should count as **one event for the regional series**, even though the 2026 championship programme was held in three stages around Sattahip and Bang Saray, Chonburi. The official notice includes Optimist, defines a three-race completion requirement, and provides an official overall Optimist result as well as Gold/Silver results. Treating the national championship as one season input avoids giving a single domestic circuit three separate opportunities to dominate the SEA table. [3] [4]

**Eastern Seaboard Youth Regatta** at Royal Varuna Yacht Club in Pattaya is the second Thai candidate. The 17th edition ran on 22–26 October 2025, drew 140 boats from 11 countries, and had 81 Optimist entries. That makes it a stronger cross-border test than a small local club regatta. Add it only when the next edition has a confirmed notice and a final result source. [5]

### Philippines — publish the result, but hold it out of the first points table

**Philippine National Sailing Championship** deserves early SailorPath coverage. The official 2026 result document records an Optimist Class A cohort of five sailors and an Optimist Class B cohort of sixteen in Manila Bay. It does not publish one compatible Optimist overall of at least 20 sailors, so it should be displayed in country and sailor histories but not score in the first SEA Series under the proposed minimum-cohort rule. Work with Philippine Sailing on a future combined Open/overall result or a clearly constituted eligible division before reconsidering it for points. [6]

### Indonesia — a promising conditional candidate

**Tukik Regatta 7th** is the Indonesian event to investigate first. Its 2026 Notice of Race scheduled racing at Tukik Anyer Surf and Sail in Banten on 10–13 October and listed Optimist A, B, and Green classes, with domestic and international participation permitted. At the time of this recommendation, a final Optimist result and field size had not been published. Keep it in the calendar/research pipeline, but do not include it in a points table until a final, compatible cohort result passes review. [7]

## Sensible first pilot calendar

If the next notices and results meet the gates, use a six-event pilot made up of two existing Singapore events, the two Malaysian anchors, the Thailand National Championship treated as one event, and the Eastern Seaboard Youth Regatta. This gives the first season a balanced, understandable schedule across three countries without pretending the platform already has complete regional coverage.

Philippine and Indonesian events should appear in the public **Southeast Asia Optimist Results** directory immediately once their sources are verified. They become points events only after they meet the same documented cohort, race-count, and final-overall requirements as every other country.

## Decisions required before implementation

1. Approve a fixed pilot season with six named events and best-three scoring.
2. Confirm the 20-sailor / four-race minimum, or set a different threshold before partner conversations begin.
3. Ask Kedah Sailing Association, the Yacht Racing Association of Thailand, Royal Varuna Yacht Club, and Philippine Sailing whether SailorPath may use their official results and appoint a review contact.
4. Decide whether SailorPath will accept only organiser-supplied results or also ingest publicly available official results subject to organiser correction.
5. Build the event-score ledger and immutable published calculation run before displaying a regional standings page.
